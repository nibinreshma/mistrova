Vaduvanchal

The junction town of Muppainad panchayat, on the road south towards the Tamil Nadu border. This sheet is a reference, not a brochure: what the town is, what sits around it, and how you reach it.

Muppainad panchayat’s offices are at Vaduvanchal. Until 2000 the area was part of Meppadi panchayat, which under the British was the most populous of the twelve special panchayats in Wayanad.

Vaduvanchal is the town for the country south of Meppadi: a junction with a bazaar, the panchayat office, and roads running on towards the Tamil Nadu border. It is twelve kilometres from Meppadi and eighteen from Ambalavayal, and almost no one arrives here who is not going somewhere further.

The land is plantation. Tea, cardamom, coffee and pepper have been grown on this ground since the 1800s, when the British estates were laid out, and the shape of the settlements still follows the estates: lines of workers’ quarters, estate roads, a bazaar where they meet. Coffee is the largest single sector in the panchayat today.

It is border country in a real sense. Muppainad touches Tamil Nadu on two sides, and Malappuram district on a third, and the roads south towards Cherambadi and the Gudalur country carry a traffic of their own that has little to do with Wayanad. The forest on those edges is continuous with the Nilgiris.

For a visitor it is a quiet alternative to the Meppadi side: the same hills, the same estates, far fewer people. The Vellarimala country to the west, the Chembra and Soochipara roads to the north, and the long drive south to the border are all reached from here.

Getting here, and getting out

Nothing in Wayanad is far. The roads are slow, so read these in hours rather than kilometres.

By road

  • FROM MEPPADI
    12 km north-west, about 25 minutes through the estates.
  • FROM AMBALAVAYAL
    18 km north, a quieter road across the plateau.
  • FROM KALPETTA
    ~27 km via Meppadi.
  • FROM TAMIL NADU
    The Cherambadi and Gudalur roads come up from the south; slow and narrow.
  • AROUND
    Buses run Meppadi–Vaduvanchal and on towards the border; jeeps work the estate roads.
